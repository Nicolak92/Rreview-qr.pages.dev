How to Calculate Landed Cost for Imported Goods

The supplier’s price is only part of what an imported product really costs you. Landed cost is the full price of getting it to your door, and you need it to price your products properly.

What landed cost includes

  • The product cost
  • Freight and insurance
  • Import duty
  • Taxes you cannot reclaim, such as VAT or GST
  • Customs and broker fees, port charges and delivery to your door

How to calculate it

  1. Open the Landed Cost Calculator and enter your unit cost and quantity.
  2. Add freight, insurance, import duty, VAT or GST, and other fees.
  3. Read the landed cost per unit and the cost breakdown.
  4. Add your selling price to see profit and margin per unit.

CIF or FOB?

Import duty is a percentage of the customs value. Many countries use goods plus freight plus insurance (CIF). Some use the goods only (FOB). Check which rule applies in your country, and check the duty rate for your product code.

Common mistakes

  • Using only the supplier price and forgetting freight and duty
  • Using the wrong customs value rule for your country
  • Counting VAT or GST as a cost when you can reclaim it
  • Forgetting port, broker and delivery fees

Frequently asked questions

Should I include VAT or GST?

Only if you cannot reclaim it. Businesses that can reclaim import VAT or GST should leave it out of their cost.

How is import duty calculated?

Duty is a percentage of the customs value. Many countries use goods + freight + insurance (CIF). Some use goods only (FOB). Choose the rule that applies to you.

Can I use any currency?

Yes. Use one currency for every field and change the symbol at the top.
