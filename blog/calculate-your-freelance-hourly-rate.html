How to Work Out Your Freelance Hourly Rate

Many freelancers set their rate by guessing, or by copying what others charge. A simple calculation shows what you really need to charge to reach your income goal.

How to calculate it

  1. Open the Freelance Hourly Rate Calculator and enter the yearly income you want to take home, and your business costs.
  2. Add your tax rate, your weekly hours and your weeks off.
  3. Set the share of your time that is billable. Be honest: most freelancers bill far less than 100%.
  4. Read your hourly rate, day rate and the price for a project.

The formula

Revenue needed = (take-home income + business costs) ÷ (1 − tax rate), plus a safety buffer. Hourly rate = revenue needed ÷ billable hours per year.

Why it is higher than an employee’s pay

An employee is paid for every working hour and gets paid time off. A freelancer pays for their own costs, taxes and time off, and cannot bill every hour. Billable time is the share of your hours you can charge for. Admin, marketing and finding clients do not count, so 50% to 70% is common.

A note on taxes

This is an estimate, not tax or financial advice. Tax rules differ by country, so ask an accountant about your own situation.

Frequently asked questions

Should I charge by the hour or by the project?

Many freelancers price by project but use their hourly rate to check the price is fair. The sample project in the tool shows that price.

Why add a safety buffer?

Work can be uneven, clients pay late and costs rise. A buffer of 10% to 20% protects you.

What should I use for tax?

Use a rough total for income tax and social charges in your country. Ask an accountant for a better number.
