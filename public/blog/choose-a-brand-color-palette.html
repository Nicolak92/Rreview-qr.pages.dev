How to Choose a Color Palette for Your Brand or Website

People react to color before they read a single word. A small, consistent palette makes your website, signs and social posts look more professional.

How many colors do you need

Three to five is plenty: a main color, a second color, a light neutral, a dark neutral, and one accent for buttons and links. Fewer colors look more confident and are easier to keep consistent.

A simple way to balance them

Many designers use the 60-30-10 rule. About 60% of the space is your main or neutral color, 30% is your second color, and 10% is the accent. It is a guide, not a law, but it stops a design from feeling busy.

Check that text is easy to read

Text needs strong contrast with its background. The WCAG accessibility guidelines recommend a contrast ratio of at least 4.5 to 1 for normal text. Dark text on a light background, or the reverse, usually passes. Light gray on white often does not.

Frequently asked questions

What is a HEX code?

A short code such as #0b5cff that tells a browser or design tool exactly which color to use.

Is the palette generator free?

Yes. It is free and needs no sign-up.

Should I use the same colors everywhere?

Yes. Using one palette on your website, social profiles and printed material makes your brand easier to recognize.
